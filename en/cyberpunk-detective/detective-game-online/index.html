    How do you solve a detective case?

    Search the scene first, then test every story against what you found: the culprit is the one whose story the evidence cannot support. A whodunit works this way: the reader gets the same chance to deduce as the detective (Wikipedia).

    Search before you listenCollect what the scene says before anyone tells you their version.
    Keep it all in one placeA diary of every clue and statement lets you compare them side by side.

    How do you catch a lie?

    Set what a person says against something that cannot be argued with: a door log, a message, a mark on a glass. A lie is a statement that one clue contradicts.

    One statement, one clueHere each suspect says one thing that a single clue breaks.
    A broken lie opens a doorFaced with the evidence, a suspect admits something new. That becomes your next clue.

    What makes a case fair?

    You see every clue the detective sees, before the accusation, and only one answer fits. Detective writers set this down as rules in 1928 and 1929 (Van Dine; Knox), and this case follows them.

    The same clues as the detectiveNothing is hidden from you: the clues are in the lab and in what people say.
    Only one answer fitsThe clues support one sentence only. Guessing does not get you there.

    Why a sentence and not just a name?

    Because naming a person is not enough: who, how and why have to fit together. Some answers stay locked until you have found the clue they depend on, so you cannot guess your way through.

    Who, how and whyThe three parts have to fit each other.
    Locked answersSome options only unlock once you have their clue.

    FAQ

    Can I play this detective game online for free?

    Yes. It runs in your browser: no download, no account, nothing to pay.

    How long does the case take?

    About eight minutes, a bit more if you read every statement.

    Is there only one solution?

    Yes. The clues fit one sentence only, and some answers stay locked until you find the clue they need.

    What happens if my deduction is wrong?

    You see how many of the three parts are right, plus a hint from ECO. The solution stays hidden and you can change your answer straight away.
