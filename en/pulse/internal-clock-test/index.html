Internal clock test: the 10 second challenge

An internal clock test asks you to judge a time with no clock to read. Press Start and the clock disappears; count 10 seconds in your head and press Stop. It is the 10 second challenge: pass within ±0.10 s, Perfect on 10.00.

The rules

10.00Perfect: dead on, to the hundredth
9.90–10.10Pass: within ±0.10 s
< 9.90 · > 10.10Miss: too early or too late

How to play the 10 second challenge

Press Start: the clock fades out. Count 10 seconds in your head and press Stop. The clock comes back and shows your time. It is the 3 second challenge with no clock to lean on.

How accurate is your internal clock?

It varies from person to person and the error is relative: in a study of timing from 68 ms to 16.7 min, the coefficient of variation (spread divided by the interval) fell slowly as intervals got longer, rather than staying constant (Lewis & Miall, 2009). Your own result is the number that matters, so try several runs.

A constant coefficient of variation is the scalar property of timing, described in animals (Gibbon, 1977). In humans the picture is less tidy, which is one reason there is no single “typical error at 10 s” to quote here.

Does counting in your head help?

Not for accuracy, but for precision: counting did not improve the accuracy of 10 to 90 s productions, but it did make tries more consistent (Thönes & Hecht, 2017). At 60 and 90 s counting also made people overshoot; that disappeared at shorter intervals.

Tips for the 10 second challenge

Pick a rhythm and keep it steady to the end.

  • Count at a steady pace. Counting makes your tries more consistent (Thönes & Hecht, 2017), so repeat and watch the spread.
  • Train with the clock first. A few tries of the 3 second challenge set your rhythm.
  • Try the opposite task. In the time estimation test you judge a gap instead of producing one.

FAQ

How accurate is your internal clock?

Does counting in your head help?

Not for accuracy: counting did not improve accuracy of 10-90 s productions, but it did improve precision (less spread between attempts) (Thönes & Hecht, 2017). At 60 and 90 s counting also made people overshoot; that disappeared at shorter intervals.

Why is the clock hidden?

Because the test is of your own time sense; with a visible clock you would read it instead.

What is the 10 second challenge?

Start a clock, hide it, and try to stop it at exactly 10.00 s. The closer you stop, the better your internal clock estimate.
