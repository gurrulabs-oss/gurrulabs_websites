Best reaction time apps for Android

Five apps compared on what their Google Play listings actually say: price, ads, modes, account and offline use. We make one of them, and say so.

Published 6 November 2026Listings checked 6 November 20263 min read
Short answer
If you only want to measure your reaction time, Reaction Time (Banensoft) is a simple reaction test plus a minigame; Reaction training (nixGames) adds 55+ reflex and puzzle challenges and a two-player mode; Pulse pairs a reaction test and Batak with two timing modes; Lumosity and Peak are general brain-training suites (Lumosity lists speed as one of its skill areas). All five are free to install; all but Lumosity show Play’s “Contains ads” label, and Lumosity offers Premium subscriptions. Full disclosure: Pulse is made by Gurrulabs, the authors of this page.

How we compared

Every fact in the table, ours included, comes from each app’s Google Play listing on the date above, nothing else. Where a listing does not say something, the table says “not stated” instead of guessing. The only exception is the Pulse card below: as its makers we add that it needs no account and works offline, which its listing does not state.

  • Price: free, free with in-app purchases, or subscription, as Play shows it.
  • Ads: Play’s “Contains ads” label.
  • Reaction modes: the kinds of test the description lists.
  • Account and offline use: only if the listing states it.

What an app cannot tell you

Phones add their own touch and screen delay: screens, mice and software can add up to 100 ms to the measured time (Woods et al., 2015). So a time in one app is not comparable to the millisecond with another app or with lab figures. Compare yourself with yourself, on the same phone. FAQ

Is there a free reaction time app with no ads?

Of the five we compared, only Lumosity has no “Contains ads” label on Google Play, and it offers Premium subscriptions. The other four, Pulse included, show the label.

Can a reflex app make me faster?

Practice makes your own average more stable. On fast choice tasks, action video game players responded 11% faster than non-players with the same accuracy, and some training studies indicate that playing can speed up responses (Dye et al., 2009). No app can promise a specific gain.

Why did you include your own app?

Because it is a reaction time app for Android. We compare it on the same criteria as the others.
