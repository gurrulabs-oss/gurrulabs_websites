Reaction time in sports

How fast athletes have to react in five sports, what the rules and studies actually measure, and which Pulse test comes closest to each one.

Published 20 November 2026Updated 20 November 20266 min read

Short answer
Sprint rules treat a reaction off the blocks under 0.100 s as a possible false start (World Athletics, 2026), so even elite athletes work near a floor. In other sports the question is less how fast and more what to choose and when: studies compare athletes on both reaction and anticipation (Nuri et al., 2013). They describe groups, not targets.

How fast is a sprinter’s reaction time?

Fast enough that the rules set a floor: a reaction off the blocks under 0.100 s is treated as a possible false start.

0.100 s

When a certified start system is used, it alerts the starter to a possible false start whenever an athlete reacts in under 0.100 s; the starter then checks the start. An analysis of World and European Championship times from 1999 to 2014 suggested revised thresholds of 115 ms for men and 119 ms for women (Brosnan et al., 2017).

World Athletics, 2026

How fast does a goalkeeper have to react to a penalty?

One study of goalkeepers proposed that reliable information becomes available late within the time constraints of a penalty kick.

Timed to the plant foot

In staged penalty kicks with the kicking distance and run-up speed varied, high-skilled goalkeepers timed the start of their dive to the landing of the kicker’s non-kicking leg next to the ball, irrespective of the time constraints.

Zheng et al., 2021

How do tennis players react to a serve?

Top professionals describe using the server’s movement and the match situation to anticipate the serve.

Anticipation

Eight current or former top-250 professional players, interviewed about returning serve, described how cues from the server’s movement and contextual information interact to shape their anticipation. The evidence is what players said, not measurements of their reactions.

Vernon et al., 2018

What is a boxer’s reaction time like?

Good reaction times but poor accuracy, in one 2025 review of EEG studies that included martial arts and boxing.

Good speed, poor accuracy

In 35 studies using EEG and cognitive tasks, most found athletes outperformed controls in accuracy and reaction time; martial arts and boxing showed good reaction times but poor accuracies.

Delgado et al., 2025

What is the reaction time of pro gamers?

No study cited here measures pros; in action video game players, choice responses were 11% faster than in non-players, with the same accuracy.

11% faster

Action game players answered fast choice tasks 11% faster with the same accuracy. It is a comparison of groups of players, not of professionals.

Dye et al., 2009

Why does sport need more than raw reaction speed?

Because most sports ask you to choose, not just to react, and experts also anticipate.

Simple reaction

One signal, one response: a sprint start.

Choice reaction

Several possible signals, a different response for each: a save, a return, a block. Choice reaction time is related to the number of alternatives (Proctor & Schneider, 2018).

In a comparison of 11 sprinters and 11 volleyball players, the sprinters did better on both auditory reaction tests and the volleyball players on both anticipation tests, with no significant difference on the two visual choice reaction tests; the authors conclude that athletes have greater sensory-cognitive skills related to their own sport (Nuri et al., 2013).

Can athletes train their reaction time?

Partly, and mostly on what they practise: in a six-week study of 12 to 16 year old field hockey players, a vision training program improved a trained choice reaction task, but not an untrained tracking task (Schwab & Memmert, 2012). FAQ

What is a good reaction time for an athlete?

There is no single number: it depends on the sport and the task. In sprinting, a reaction under 0.100 s off the blocks is flagged as a possible false start (World Athletics, 2026).

Do athletes have faster reaction times than other people?

In a 2025 review of 35 studies, most found athletes did better than non-athletes in accuracy and reaction time (Delgado et al., 2025). These are group comparisons and promise nothing to any individual.

Which sport needs the fastest reactions?

No study we checked ranks sports. In one comparison, sprinters did better than volleyball players on auditory reaction tests and the volleyball players did better on anticipation tests (Nuri et al., 2013).

Are gamers’ reactions as fast as athletes’?

No study cited here compares them directly. Action video game players responded 11% faster than non-players on fast choice tasks (Dye et al., 2009).
